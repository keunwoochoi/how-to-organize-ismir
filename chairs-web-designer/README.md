# Web & Design Chairs

## General Guidelines (Timeless)

### Goal
Provide the primary information hub for the conference. It must be accessible, up-to-date, and mobile-friendly.

### Core Responsibilities
*   **Website**: Design, build, and maintain the conference website.
*   **Assets**: Create visual assets (logos, banners) for other chairs.
*   **Updates**: Rapidly publish news (CFPs, keynotes, schedule).

### Mental Models
*   **"The Living Document"**: The website is never "done". It changes weekly. Build it to be easily updatable.
*   **"Mobile First"**: Attendees will check the schedule on their phones while running to the next session.

### Timeline
*   **Month -9**: Launch "Save the Date" page.
*   **Month -6**: Full site launch (CFPs, keynotes).
*   **Month -2**: Schedule & Registration integration.
*   **Month +1**: Transition to "Archive" mode.

## 2025 Specifics (Reference)

### Decisions
*   **Platform**: Custom site (GitHub Pages + Jekyll).
    *   *Why*: Free hosting, version control, easy for devs.
*   **Analytics**: Google Analytics.
    *   *Usage*: Periodic reports to GCs.
*   **Post-Conference**: Transitioned to "Archive" mode (Awards, Proceedings links) 2 weeks after.

### Lessons Learned
*   **"Stay Tuned" is Bad**: Don't leave "Stay Tuned" on the homepage after the conference. Switch immediately to "Highlights" or "Thank You".
*   **Asset Repository**: Keep a shared folder of logos/colors for other chairs (Social Media, Local Org) to use.
*   **Archive Strategy**: Plan the post-conference state *before* the conference ends. You'll be too tired afterwards.
