# General Chairs

Unlike other docs, this doc is fully manually written by Keunwoo, Juhan, Dasaem.

## How to define chairing areas and appoint chairs

Initially, we reviewed the previous years' chairs and the area. Some areas seem obvious (e.g. LBD chairs, publication chairs) while some other areas were slightly different every year, and we thought that was enough. For example, ismir 2024 shows 40 members in the committee from general chairs to logo design.

We were wrong. It may be true that some of the chairs had little works to do. That doesn't mean all the works we would do were going to be fully covered by them. We did it with 37 chairs, but there are areas where some chairs had to do a lot of work, or where the general chairs had to fill the gap. Neither is great, and should be avoided as much as possible.

It is also a great idea to set a goal for the general chairs to NOT busy during the last minute and the conference. In other words, everything that can be foreseen should be delegated, by adding new areas and new chairs; and the general chairs should be ready to make decision & possibly work on it for unpredictable tasks, since during last minutes, only general chairs can own those tasks and make quick decisions.

## Suggestions on Chairs

### General Chairs 

We are glad that we did it with three general chairs. It could've been two, but Keunwoo joined a year before the conference, and that helped. Even if Keunwoo was on remote (from New York) unlike others (in Seoul and Daejeon & the conference was at Daejeon), surely many things can be done remotely, just like many other chairs.

We strongly believe it was great to have chairs from the academia, simply because ISMIR is an academic conference, and hosting it is easily aligned with the goal of those in academia than industry. Thanks to Juhan's and Dasaem's affiliations, it was possible to save the budget for the venue of ISMIR and other satelite events. 

### Professional Conference Organizers (PCOs)

It is common to contract with a PCO, as they can take care of all the non-core tasks such as catering, booking venues for other events such as jam session and banquet, issuing visa-supporting docs, as well as registration (websites and payments). We did the same, after carefully screening multiple options and their costs.

PCOs don't always think and deliever as we want. Even if they might be experienced with other conferences, ISMIR is different from the average academic conferences: We care a lot. This resulted in lots of adjustments we've made to their initial suggestions, e.g. coffee available for whole day / the attendance ratio to reception and banquet is higher / more options for food / beter handling various names in alphabet etc / giving much more generous and diverse grants / support the sponsors better / organize the social event with more care. If we didn't tell them, they wouldn't have done them for us, and according to our survey, this has caused some unsatisfying conference experience in some years.

The suggestion is not to naively fully trust them. A micro-management is necessary. 


### Satellite Event Chairs

In some years, we didn't have it, including 2025. But looking back, it would've been better to have one. One of the general chairs did this work in 2025, which contributed to their overworking. 


### Scientific Program Chairs
We had 4 chairs, and we believe it was really necesary to have all of them.

At least one of them should do some technical works, mostly simple data engineering with spreadsheets. After the reviewing period, they would be really busy on this work. We absolutely recommend finding people in academia. 

### Award Chairs

Now that we have best papers and test-of-time awards, one has to take responsible for defining committee(s) and leading the conclusion. This is related to SPCs but due to their work load, it would be nice to have a separate person for this. One of the general chairs did it in 2025, and it was a bit risky to deliver this on time. 

### Publication Chair

In 2025 and 2024, there was one person in this area, and it seems fine. Can be from industry, but again, someone from academia would be preferred -- at least in our case of 2025, that seems to have contributed to the smooth work in this area.

### Tutorial Chairs

It is usually 2-3 people, and it seems fine with it. One person wouldn't be ideal, not because of the amount of the work but for the diversity. Selecting 6 tutorials in various area is a challenging task, so better to choose 2+ people from different backgrounds. A mixture of industry and academia would work well. 

Tutorial chairing is one of the rare areas where most of the work can be done weeks before the conference. But it would be ideal if they can attend the conference in-person, since some supports might be needed at the tutorial, and volunteers are not always capable of handling those tasks.

### LBD Chairs

The traiditon of 2-3 people semes working well. Similar to the publication chair, they would need to work on the submission platform and handle some spreadsheets. Similar to tutorial, a mixture of industry and academia would work well. During the LBD submission, they would be actually pretty busy reviewing the submissions, which is short but a lot altogether. Therefore, it's important to ensure such time availabilities.


### Music Chairs

The ISMIR Music Program didn't always happen, and a similar task may be defined in each year. For similar tasks, it would have 2 music chairs rather than one, for the same reason with LBD and tutorial - that they would need to somewhat evaluate the submissions, and it would be much better balanced to have two people. The workload is relatively small.


### Sponsorship / Industry

In 2025, these two tasks were done by separate chairs (1 and 2 people, respectively). 
**Separate or Together**: it would make sense to combine this task. The sponsoring companies are eligible to participate the industry session, so the communications overlap. It didn't cause much trouble to have separate chairs in 2025, partly since they knew each other very well already; also the overlapped part (e.g. sharing the point of contact from Sponsorship chair to Industry chairs;) is not a huge amount of work. But it requires quick communication and decision making.

**How many people**: At least 2, and if combined, 3 would also make sense. Getting sponsorship itself is a full, one person's task through the preparing months. 

People affiliated at the industry have done this task, which makes sense -- they tend to know more people in the industry, they probably understand the decision making process behind the scene, and they might benefit personally more directly by doing this task.


### Virtual Chairs
Setting up the miniconf website, preparing and handling the Slack workspace, and managing YouTube channel is a lot of work! They might be somewhat unfairly tasked as well, e.g. getting materials (papers, posters, and videos) shouldn't be their job but they may feel obliged to chase the authors just because they are those who use them to do their work. They also become busy during the conference due to last minute requests on adding people to Slack, updating materials, etc. 

**How many**: 3 would be better than 2. Again, it's a lot of work.

**Who**: This requires some devoted hours. Students might be better suited, and even so, the 2025 general chairs agreed to send some gift cards to the student virtual chairs who did a lot of development works. We believe it's the only fair way, because doing this work does not really give any direct benefit e.g. connecting to other people. We still believe though, that this would be done better by the ISMIR society members than outsourced companies; due to the particular aspects of the work (e.g. miniconf or slack: only those who have attended ISMIR and other virtual conferences would assume the requirements correctly.)

**Time zone consideration**: We originally thought it would be important to cover different time zones. But turned out most of the work would be done before the conference. During the conference, it was okay to respond to some requests with few hours of delay. So, it would be nice to cover perhaps two time zones, but doesn't have to cover the whole timezones across the world.

### Grant Chairs
We had two chairs, one from local and another who is experienced ISMIR attendees. This combination worked very well, as the local one had to directly contact hotels / PCOs on many many details, while the ISMIR-experienced one were able to provide important perspectives to consider during the grant selection process.

We gave perhaps the highest numbers of grant in 2025, and this resulted in a lot of work to both of the chairs, approaching the conference. This part of work should be communicated with the PCO accurately, so that the grant chair wouldn't be overworking. 

### Newcomer Initiative Chair

It is a nice task that can be done by one person, who is enthusiastic on onboarding newcomers. The chair should attend the conference perhaps in-person to support the related activities. 

### Web Chair / Design

The workload of Web Chair is a lot, since the website needs to be constantly updated for several months. We believe it was our best decision to find a student that one of us supervises, who is an actually professional visual artist with extensive skill on web design. Similar decisions seem to have been made for the same reason. 

In 2025, we decided to pay the web chair due to the nature of the job and considering the market range. It was still more cost-effective for us compared to hiring a contractor, as well as easier communication. 

### Local Organization Chairs

In 2025, the seven local organization chairs have done a lot of work in various areas. In the past, the job has been also allocated to other chairs (tech chairs, virtual logistics chairs, etc) as well as the PCO. But in 2018, there were 14 local organizers. 

**Catering and event planning**: Although many jobs can be done by the PCO, someone in the committee should own this task and make decisions, to provide satisfying experience to the conference attendees. In 2025, this included all the snacks, drinks, welcome reception, banquet, and K-culture night. 

**Technical Rehearsal and Preparation**: It was necessary to spend time, before the confernce, on technical rehearsal for presenting, recording, and livestreaming of tutorial and oral sessions. ISMIR is forced to provide flexibilities on presentation materials (e.g. we can't stick with Google Slides only.) 

**Handling Presentation Materials**: Besides rehearsal, all the slide decks in pdf, pptx, keynote, and google slides should be perfectly compiled for each session, and there's always chance some attendees would want to make last minute changes. This circumstance makes this task constantly demanding through the 4 days.

**Shuttle Bus & Any Others**: 2025 might be an extreme example with shuffle buses available every day. But some sort of transportation has been often needed in other years, too.

### Volunteer Chair

A lot of autonomy is needed in this area. The volunteer chairs should handle some unexpected situations by themselves in a timely manner and should be a fast communicator. At least one local chair is preferred to fully understand the tasks and assign people appropriately.

The demand for volunteers is somewhat difficult to fully anticipate. Volunteers are also often newcomers to ISMIR, and it may be related to grants. That is very nice of us as a society, but unfortunately, that sometimes makes it difficult to assign tasks to volunteers. 


### Social Media Chair

This is a somewhat lightweight task, and can be done remotely. Our social media activity is single-directional, so anyone who can plan and execute sharing scheduled messages can do it. But surely some passion in ISMIR would be necessary, as the task may be self-defined and we would still want to do a good job. 

### Unconference Chair

The unconference itself seems to be considered as a bonus session of the conference. Somehow, Geoffroy has been the only chair through 2023, 2024, and 2025. This may mean something is missing in this area. It indeed seems like an under-defined session to the 2025 general chairs.

### 2) Content Streaming and Recording
- Context: Ensure high-quality delivery and archiving.
- Decision owner(s): Virtual Chairs (coordinate with Session Chairs, Publication Chairs).
- Options: Live only vs recorded; automated vs manual; streaming platform choice; archiving strategy.
- 2025 decision: Live with recordings; manual moderation; YouTube for livestreaming and archiving.
- Pros/Cons: Recordings increase access; live builds excitement. YouTube provides excellent rewind/replay capabilities for latecomers and persistent archiving.
- Recommendations: Use reliable streams; edit for on-demand. Post-conference archiving strategy: Publish session-level videos (oral sessions, special sessions, keynotes) + separate long talks (high-scoring papers) with proper titles for discoverability. Critical: obtain consent BEFORE conference for all recordings/uploads – much easier than retroactive requests.
