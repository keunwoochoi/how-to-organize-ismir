# General Chairs

## General Guidelines (Timeless)

### Goal
The General Chairs (GCs) are the ultimate decision-makers and safety net for the conference. Your goal is to oversee the entire organization, fill in gaps, and ensure the conference reflects the ISMIR community's values.

### Mental Models
*   **"The Gap Filler"**: If a chair is missing or underperforming, you are the backup. You must be ready to step in.
*   **"Delegate Everything Foreseeable"**: If you can predict a task, assign it to a chair. Save your bandwidth for the unpredictable crises that *only* you can handle.
*   **"Academic vs. Industry Balance"**: ISMIR is an academic conference. Having GCs from academia helps align with the mission, but industry connections are vital for funding.

### Timeline & Milestones
*   **Month -18 to -12**: Form the core team (Local, Finance, SPC).
*   **Month -12**: Contract PCO (Professional Conference Organizer).
*   **Month -6**: Launch website and calls for participation.
*   **Month -1**: Crisis management mode.

### Working with PCOs
*   **Trust but Verify**: PCOs handle logistics (catering, registration), but they don't know ISMIR's culture.
*   **Micro-management**: You *must* micro-manage details like dietary options, student support, and sustainability. Standard corporate conference defaults are often insufficient for ISMIR.

### Team Composition & Recruitment
Appointing the right people is your most critical task. A bad chair choice creates a hole you have to fill.

*   **Scientific Program Chairs (SPC)**
    *   **Profile**: Senior or mid-career academics with extensive reviewing experience. Must be detail-oriented and willing to make tough decisions.
    *   **Why**: They handle the core content. If they fail, the scientific quality drops.
    *   **Warning**: Avoid "Idea Guys" who don't like email. This is an admin-heavy role.
*   **Local Organization Chair**
    *   **Profile**: Local resident, speaks the local language fluently, knows the venue intimately.
    *   **Why**: They handle the PCO, catering, and on-site emergencies. They are your "boots on the ground".
*   **Finance Chair**
    *   **Profile**: Extremely trustworthy, spreadsheet-literate, and patient with bureaucracy.
    *   **Why**: They handle the budget, bank transfers, and reimbursements. A mistake here can bankrupt the conference.
*   **Sponsorship / Industry Chairs**
    *   **Profile**: Good networker, persistent, "sales" mindset. Often from industry or a senior academic with industry ties.
    *   **Why**: They bring in the money. They need to be comfortable asking for funds and chasing invoices.
*   **Web / Tech Chairs**
    *   **Profile**: Actual developers (frontend/backend). Not just "good with computers".
    *   **Why**: They build and maintain the website and systems. If the site goes down during registration, you have a crisis.
*   **Music / Arts Chairs**
    *   **Profile**: Artist-researchers who understand both the aesthetic and technical requirements of music performance.
    *   **Why**: They curate the concert program. They need to communicate technical riders to the local AV team.
*   **Volunteer Chairs**
    *   **Profile**: People-oriented, organized, patient. Good at managing large groups.
    *   **Why**: They manage the workforce. If volunteers are unhappy, operations crumble.
*   **DEI / Grant Chairs**
    *   **Profile**: Empathetic, fair, but also rigorous with paperwork.
    *   **Why**: They handle sensitive data and financial aid. They need to balance compassion with budget limits.
*   **Virtual / Hybrid Chairs**
    *   **Profile**: Tech-savvy, "DevOps" mindset. Comfortable with OBS, Zoom, and streaming setups.
    *   **Why**: This is a high-stress, high-workload role. It requires technical problem-solving in real-time.

## 2025 Specifics (Reference)

### Core Team
*   **Chairs**: 3 General Chairs (Keunwoo, Juhan, Dasaem).
*   **Remote Chair**: Keunwoo joined late and worked remotely (NYC). This worked but required strong local coordination.
*   **Missing Roles**: We didn't have a Satellite Chair or a separate Awards Chair. GCs had to fill these gaps, leading to overwork.

### Chairing Decisions
*   **Web Chair**: We paid the Web Chair in 2025. The workload (custom design, constant updates) justified it.
*   **Virtual Chairs**: We gave gift cards to student chairs. The role is high-labor/low-networking, so compensation is fair.
*   **Sponsorship & Industry**: We had separate chairs. It worked because they communicated well, but combining these roles is also a valid strategy.

### Lessons Learned
*   **Satellite Chair is Mandatory**: Don't try to do it yourself.
*   **Local Grant Chair is Critical**: The "Global" Grant Chair can select awardees, but you need a "Local" Chair to handle the PCO/Bank interface for payments.
*   **Unconference Needs Love**: The Unconference has been under-defined for 3 years. It needs a rethink or a dedicated champion.
